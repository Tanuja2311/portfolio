      The real laps 16 to 23, replayed exactly as they happened. You're on the Ferrari pit wall, and Rosso, a scripted AI agent, works for you. It watches the same data you have, changes the view, and makes calls. Then it checks each call against what really happened on the day. It can't change the race. You decide how much it's allowed to do.

      The race never changes

      The agent controls the view and makes calls. The cars do exactly what they did on the day, so every call gets checked against what really happened, with the real call made on the day shown next to it.

      Risk decides what it asks about

      On "Ask first" it changes the view by itself, with an undo, but stops and waits for you before any race call. On "Suggest" it never touches anything. On "Act" it does both, with a few seconds to undo.

      It only knows what the pit wall knew

      Every call uses data up to that moment. When it can't measure something, it says so instead of guessing.

      Your settings win

      Pick a camera yourself and the agent stops moving it. Stop the agent and it does nothing else, but the trail keeps everything it already did.

      How Rosso rates its confidence

      Rosso rates its own confidence in every call: high, medium or low. You don't rate anything, you approve or reject. It's a fixed rule, not a feeling, so you only need to read this once.

      HighThe call follows directly from numbers already measured.
      MediumThe call depends on something that can't be measured yet, like how the track or the tyres will behave.
